# Independent sensory-input boundary review for a grounded attention bridge

Verdict: a new grounded attention bridge may exclude the failed sensory correctness calibrator entirely and learn its own forecasts from admissible frozen sensory features, raw task logits, action histories and executed outcomes. A separately qualified stand-alone correctness calibrator is not a logical prerequisite when none of its probabilities enters the bridge, Controller, Reporter or evaluation target. This verdict does not override any previous failed gate, approve the new bridge, or establish appearance correspondence.

## Scope and provenance

This is an explicitly unblinded AI methodological review after the parent reported the sensory adapter outcome: highest-contrast classification above 99%, lowest positive contrast around 90%, zero contrast near chance, and overall engineering failure due to conditional calibration of the two-parameter margin instrument. These numbers are parent summaries, not independently audited results here. The reviewer did not read raw new outputs, recompute outcomes, run checkpoints, fit models, tune a replacement calibrator or inspect a new bridge implementation. The earlier pre-output sensory adapter review provides architectural context. The reviewer shares model/training and commissioning context with the parent; this is not human validation or external replication.

## Why the boundary is valid

A raw sensory logit is an uncalibrated feature. It can carry useful predictive information without already constituting a valid probability of correctness. An attention bridge trained on executed action/delivery/task outcomes can learn a different conditional prediction function that incorporates history and action effects. Its validity depends on its own held-out outcome qualification, rather than on a discarded intermediate mapping. Calibration of a particular absolute-margin function is therefore sufficient for using that function as a probability instrument, but not necessary for using its underlying sensory features.

The complete sensory adapter protocol still failed. Its successful classification component may be used only as a frozen, explicitly partial task prerequisite with that limitation disclosed. No claim may silently turn the conjunction of old gates into a pass. The new bridge has a different target and requires a new pre-output registration, independent review, fresh split family and separately retained verdict. It must not be an indirectly renamed margin-calibrator retry with the same probability endpoint and evaluation set.

## Required qualification boundaries

1. Define observable targets before fitting. Distinguish actual delivery/acquisition, correctness of an executed sensory decision, and useful correct access such as delivery AND correctness. Specify how absent deliveries, abstentions, memory age, guessed responses and delayed outcomes are scored. Do not call delivery probability correctness, or either one clarity or appearance.
2. Freeze the sensory classifier/features and prohibit the failed calibrator from all consumers. Truth labels and realized future outcomes are supervision/evaluation only. Explicitly define which sensory features are available at each time; do not give a history-based bridge inaccessible future images, unrevealed object features or oracle correctness. If all-world features are architecturally available, disclose that information and avoid claiming a restricted acquisition channel that the implementation bypasses.
3. Use fresh whole-episode fit/calibration/evaluation splits. Keep dependent objects/actions/delays together. Fit the bridge against actual executed outcomes, with equal-budget admissible source alternatives. Qualify its own probability forecasts by prespecified action, sensory condition, delivery/history and delay strata with adequate support, reliability/Brier/residual assessment and grouped uncertainty. A favorable pooled score cannot conceal failure in the conditions used by the Controller or Reporter.
4. Counterfactual action/effect forecasts require coverage. Use randomized or otherwise justified exploration with sufficient action support, or validated controlled simulator interventions that hold the pre-action state fixed and execute each alternative. Observational prediction under a selected policy alone does not certify counterfactual accuracy. An intervention must alter the claimed state while preserving the sensory/Controller alternatives intended to be controlled, and restoration must restore the relevant forecast/behavior.
5. Separate fitting and evaluation roles. The bridge predicts outcomes; the Controller chooses/executes actions; the Reporter reads specified model state. Their targets and inputs must remain explicit. Compare raw sensory, task-decision, history/action and attention-state sources fairly before claiming model-specific causal/reporting information. Preserve every model, condition and failure without selecting a favorable checkpoint or changing thresholds after outputs.

## Implication for the project goal

This route avoids treating a failed margin calibration as a mandatory architecture component. It can establish a grounded attention/acquisition model and distinguish accurate reports of that model from inaccurate narration. It cannot by itself establish that the model supplies qualia. Independently grounded consciousness-related correspondence, selective model interventions/restoration, sensory/Controller/Reporter alternatives, confirmation and broader replication remain necessary. Neither successful task accuracy nor calibrated outcome forecasting substitutes for that theory-facing evidence.
