## Does the manner condition test the intended hypothesis?

The stringent condition is legitimate for its own claim: coarse-order retention,
entropy changes, scalar-matched coordinate permutations and targeting effects
do not distinguish an additional fine-location distribution from a separate
representational manner. Calling coarse order the whole content would settle
that question by definition. V1 has not supplied an independent positive warrant
for its strict manner identification, and that negative must remain.

However, the user's broader reporting hypothesis does not logically require a
property disjoint from every finer content, confidence and access description.
If 'all fine content' includes the complete role/location probability fields,
holding that content fixed holds this candidate form fixed too. Every observable
difference can then be redescribed as extra content or changed access, excluding
relational source contents before correspondence is tested. Scalar/explanatory
alternatives should delimit the claim, not define every possible qualified
content organization as ineligible. This concern identifies a possible mismatch
between a stringent identification claim and the goal; it does not demonstrate
that v1 manner actually passes or that such contents are phenomenal.

A new interpretation must make this distinction explicit before any Reporter
outcomes. The defensible prospective question is whether independently grounded,
addressed relational estimates and attention-effect organization supply faithful,
reference-recognizable report structure. Fine location, uncertainty and access
can be measured parts of that source organization; they are not silently relabelled
experience. A strict independent-manner claim and a relational-content correspondence
claim must have separately recorded outcomes. The first can remain negative while
the second is tested and can also fail.
