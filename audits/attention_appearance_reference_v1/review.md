# Attention and appearance reference discovery v1

Reviewer: `/root/attention_appearance_reference`. Discovery date: 2026-10-10 UTC. This is a source-discovery and design review, not an empirical human/model comparison or archive qualification. The commissioning question is whether independently grounded appearance judgments can constrain the hypothesis that an attention-control model generates qualia. No participant rows, stimulus files, or native experiment code were acquired. No human contact occurred.

## Findings

There are primary empirical studies that manipulate attention while measuring stimulus appearance. Several advertise public archives. None of their trial archives was accessible in this environment, so **zero candidates qualify for an executable held-out correspondence endpoint yet**. This changes the feasible next design: contrast-only visibility data is no longer the sole discovered reference, but acquisition, schema verification, licensing, and native-stimulus qualification remain necessary.

Do not prescribe the answer “attention makes stimuli clearer.” The discovered literature includes opposing interpretations and sign reversals. Decision confidence, calibrated classifier correctness, visibility, relative apparent contrast, reconstruction similarity, and qualia are distinct targets. An attention cue is an experimental assignment; it does not directly reveal a participant’s internal attention model.

## Candidate ledger

**Zhang and Cai (2026), attention type × contrast appearance.** The primary publisher record describes presaccadic, exogenous, and endogenous conditions; low/high stimulus contrast; and an equality judgment measure. It reports enhancement versus attenuation depending on contrast and attention type. Data and analysis code are advertised at [OSF qgak4](https://osf.io/qgak4/), and the record states that the study was not preregistered. This is a promising narrow orientation/contrast bridge candidate because a fixed positive attention multiplier would miss the reported reversal. Full native methods, row schema, stimulus code, uncertainty ratings, and archive license are unverified. The direct publisher open returned 403; indexed primary-publisher content was readable. OSF landing and API opens returned internal errors. [Primary publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0010027725002847); DOI [10.1016/j.cognition.2025.106343](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2025.106343).

**Itthipuripat and colleagues (2023), attention × fully crossed contrast with EEG and response-bias controls.** The primary article describes left/right exogenous cues, fully crossed stimulus contrasts including zero, and orientation-contingent comparative appearance judgments. Its linking models distinguish appearance-related and cue-bias terms rather than treating every cue-dependent judgment as perceptual change. [OSF 4kmcv](https://osf.io/4kmcv/) is explicitly linked for supplementary ADAN/LRP analyses and results. That link alone does **not** establish that all behavioral trial rows or native stimulus code are publicly deposited. No separate visibility/confidence rating target was verified. The institutionally deposited author paper was readable; OSF landing/API failed. This is a useful bias-control design reference, provisionally a data candidate. [Primary author manuscript](https://escholarship.org/content/qt58v116xc/qt58v116xc.pdf); DOI [10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2192-22.2023](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2192-22.2023).

**Schneider (2025), attention/bias manipulations with appearance decisions and uncertainty reports.** The primary publisher record and PubMed abstract describe comparative and equality judgments plus uncertainty in eleven participants. Data, stimuli, and analysis code are advertised at [OSF f3stc](https://osf.io/f3stc/). This could supply a particularly useful decision/appearance/uncertainty separation, rather than a target selected only for a positive appearance effect. Trial fields and license are unverified; OSF landing/API failed. [Primary publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022249625000203); [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40161011/); DOI [10.1016/j.jmp.2025.102919](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmp.2025.102919).

A published **corrigendum must accompany any use of this candidate**: the original entropy expression omitted the complementary binary-probability term. The author reports no qualitative conclusion changes and points to corrected results at [OSF hveac_v4](https://osf.io/hveac_v4). That corrected version was not retrievable here. Do not implement the original printed formula or assume the archive already contains the correction. [Primary corrigendum](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12707365/); DOI [10.1016/j.jmp.2025.102951](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmp.2025.102951).

**Horikawa and Kamitani (2022), object-based attention and appearance matching.** The primary paper instructs observers to attend to one of two superimposed natural images and separately measures remembered appearance by adjusting relative image contrast after a noise mask. It advertises raw fMRI at [OpenNeuro ds003430](https://openneuro.org/datasets/ds003430), training data at [ds001506 v1.3.1](https://openneuro.org/datasets/ds001506/versions/1.3.1), and preprocessed/source graph data at [figshare 13474629](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.13474629). The paper PDF was readable. Figshare/API and OpenNeuro retrieval failed. This is a broader content-selection reference, but not an established full attention × sensory-strength factorial. Appearance matching is retrospective, and instructions encouraged clearer perception of the attended image. Source graph data need not contain unaggregated behavioral rows. Native task code and participant-level appearance files remain unverified. [Primary paper PDF](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-021-02975-5.pdf); DOI [10.1038/s42003-021-02975-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-021-02975-5).

**Schneider and Malik (2021), adversarial interpretation reference.** The primary study uses a three-response higher/lower/equal task. Its authors interpret the population pattern as changes in criteria, without a remaining appearance effect. The instructions encouraged equality responses when observers were uncertain. No public trial-level archive or native code was established in this search. The indexed primary article was readable, but direct PMC opens encountered its browser check. Preserve this competing account during target selection and analysis; neither forced-choice shifts nor equality responses automatically identify perceptual change. [Primary paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8164366/); DOI [10.1167/jov.21.5.30](https://doi.org/10.1167/jov.21.5.30).

**Chaisilprungraung and colleagues (2026), excluded from immediately executable public-data candidates.** The primary arXiv preprint studies training under attentional competition and comparative/equality appearance judgments. Its availability section offers data and code by request, rather than a public archive. No outreach was authorized or attempted. It may inform a later design, but it cannot unblock empirical correspondence under the current access constraints. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08285); [Availability statement, PDF p. 22](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08285).

## Access verification and remaining qualification

Shell HTTPS requests to OSF node metadata, figshare article metadata, and OpenNeuro failed with `Temporary failure in name resolution`; no response body or archive bytes were downloaded. Browser errors are access outcomes for this environment, not evidence that the deposits are absent or private. GitHub discovery leads were checked only using authenticated `gh api`; both requests failed connecting to `api.github.com`. The Pan/Cai supersaturation repository lead was obtained from an unqualified secondary listing and is retained only as a lead in metadata. The Linares sensory/decision-bias repository was advertised in its author paper; it is a methods-control lead rather than a demonstrated appearance/attention dataset. No GitHub page or raw endpoint was read through the browser.

To admit a reference, acquire immutable bytes, record versions and archive/data licenses, verify participant/session/trial units, cue assignments, native sensory parameters, task wording, responses, any confidence/visibility fields, exclusions and missingness, and reproduce its stated endpoint including corrections. Do not substitute aggregate figure values for trial rows or manufacture latent attention-model states for participants. Lack of separate confidence ratings in a contrast-appearance task is a limitation to record, not grounds to invent them.

## Recommendation for the model experiment

Prioritize archive qualification of **both a contrast-dependent appearance candidate and a decision/uncertainty control candidate**; do not select among them based on current model fit. Preserve hypotheses predicting enhancement, attenuation, criterion shifts, and null effects. Freeze the external endpoint and its validity limits before measuring model correspondence.

The isolated orientation classifier can validate sensory information and provide actual decision outcomes. It cannot by itself instantiate apparent contrast, visibility, or qualia. Ground any attention/acquisition bridge in observed sensory outcomes independently of human appearance targets. Then compare upstream sensory state, attention-control model state including bound content/access relations, controller decision state, and reporter outputs with comparable information and development budgets. Source interventions and restoration should be planned separately from cue manipulations, since the human cue acts on the whole processing system. A match to a behavioral appearance target would constrain model function; it would still require a stated inferential bridge to support an experiential interpretation.
