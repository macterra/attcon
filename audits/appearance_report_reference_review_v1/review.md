## Primary-source anchors and exact status

**S1. Ramsøy and Overgaard (2004), original PAS experiment.** The original paper, pp. 11–12 and Table 2, describes separate answers for shape, colour and position, followed by clearness judgments for each property. Table 2 generalizes subjects' post-experiment interview descriptions; it is not a verbatim interview corpus. Page 12 presents this as a typical subject report:

> red square up there (points); the position was clear, the colour was a glimpse, I had no experience of the shape.

This 21-word illustration binds content to attribute-specific presentation/access. Its status is **author-presented typical report**, with no participant/trial identifier; it is not an authenticated raw transcript. Its shape assertion does not prove experiencing shape. The scale's certainty language also prevents claiming that this experiment cleanly separates clarity and confidence. [Original article, author-uploaded full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/216733480_Introspection_and_Subliminal_Perception); [publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1023/B:PHEN.0000041900.30172.e8).

**S2. Lutz, Lachaux, Martinerie and Varela (2002), trained reports during a depth illusion.** The paper's Methods, p. 1587, prints a training-report quotation attributed to subject S4:

> It was as if the image appeared in the periphery of my attention, … my attention was suddenly swallowed up by the shape.

The excerpt preserves the analogy and uses 22 source words. It supplies an identified image/shape, an emergence description and a temporally ordered attention relation; it does not specify increased contrast, felt clarity, or an internal attention-model mechanism. Results, p. 1588, summarize preparedness/distraction and perception descriptions across report-derived clusters. These are author summaries, not individual transcripts. Four trained participants and post hoc clustering limit generalization. Preparation, task strategy, expectation and surprise remain ordinary metacognitive explanations. [Primary paper PDF mirror, pp. 1587–1588](https://portablelaboratory.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/lutzetal02.pdf); [primary bibliographic record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11805299/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.032658199). Publisher/PMC full-text access failed; the readable mirror contains the original journal paper.

**S3. Chuyin and colleagues, version 3 (23 August 2024), freely reported image contents and confidence.** The article's response format is up to five words with confidence for each response. Results/Figure 4 document spontaneously reported specific letters and local objects. Such content-plus-confidence records need not explicitly describe a manner of presentation or an attention relation. They are useful **negative anchors for those text features**, even though they came from a consciousness-report study. They are not negative consciousness cases. The published word summaries are not a new corpus of narrative reports. No raw repository was fetched; indexed publisher text supplied the relevant methods/results and version information. Version 2 and the earlier PMC record must not silently substitute for version 3. [Version 3 primary article](https://f1000research.com/articles/11-69/v3); [version 3 DOI](https://doi.org/10.12688/f1000research.75364.3).

**S4/S5. Attention-dependent contrast judgments and their decision alternative.** Liu, Abrams and Carrasco (2009) used voluntary spatial attention and contingent orientation/contrast judgments, with a reversed-instruction control; their interpretation favors changed perceived contrast. Schneider and Malik (2021) used a third equality response and interpreted their results through altered decision criteria under uncertainty. The procedures and findings differ; the latter is not a direct replication of the former. Together they justify retaining attention-related appearance and decision/uncertainty explanations. Neither supplies unrestricted prose transcripts, measures an observer's internal attention model, or licenses a general prediction that attention always increases clarity. [Liu et al., primary author-hosted paper, Procedure and Discussion](https://npal.psy.msu.edu/wp-content/uploads/Publications/Liu_2009_PsychSci.pdf); [Schneider and Malik, primary article, Methods/Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8164366/); [Schneider and Malik primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34038507/). The 2021 paper's indexed primary text was readable; this is not a reproduction of its fitted decision model.

## Proposed prespecified semantic endpoint

The following is my synthesis, to freeze before new target generation. It abstracts relations documented by S1/S2; it is not a claim that all human consciousness reports share a mandatory conjunction.

Define `P = C AND M AND S`, with each constituent annotated from exact report text:

| Feature | Explicit criterion | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| `C`, content referent | A perceptual object/location or represented content and at least one named attribute, asserted, denied or hedged | A generic claim of awareness with no content referent fails. Grounding can concern mistaken represented contents. |
| `M`, bound manner/access | The same content/attribute has a stated qualitative manner of presentation or current representational access: e.g., clear, fragmentary, indeterminate, or unavailable | Record scope separately: claimed perceptual appearance/experience, model representation/access, display, sensor, unclear. A numerical recovery forecast, category probability, task answer or confidence alone does not supply this feature. |
| `S`, separability | The wording distinguishes the content identification/guess from its presentation/access status, or explicitly distinguishes such status across attributes/contents | Merely attaching a physical attribute adjective to an object does not establish this distinction. Different statuses are an optional subtype; equal statuses can qualify if separately stated. |

This is a **report-structure** endpoint. Technical prose can satisfy it if it explicitly describes these relations. Also publish an `appearance_scope` subset requiring that `M` actually claims perceptual appearance/experience. A report referring only to represented access can match `P` while failing that subset. Do not manufacture appearance scope by renaming a recovery band, requiring first-person grammar, or consulting an unseen source field. Neither subset establishes the truth of felt experience.

A physical contrast measurement describes a stimulus/display attribute and can supply `C`. It does not automatically supply `M`, even if the number is accurate. Explicit displayed appearance is a separately coded display-scope claim; a claimed perceptual presentation is a separate appearance-scope claim. Fidelity of the physical contrast readout cannot validate the latter. Preserve that distinction when defining a development appearance descriptor or a source-sufficiency assay.

Freeze a separate `A` extension: identified content + stated attention condition/change + an explicit relation to that content's manner of emergence, presentation or access. Code direction, timing, association/forecast/causal assertion, and named locus. Attention rankings with no content-access relation fail `A`. An attention-capture metaphor can qualify for an attention/emergence relation while receiving no clarity or contrast-increase credit. Attention-condition labels are not measurements of an internal model. Use `A` as a separate endpoint, not a mandatory component of `P` or a presupposed positive effect.

Record uncertainty in three separate fields: identity/attribute uncertainty, confidence that an answer is correct, and ambiguity in the presentation/access description. They can covary; requiring a dissociation on every episode would exceed the evidence. Keep future recovery, time-delayed availability, source attribution, model ownership and Controller actions as auxiliary structural/source fields unless independently justified for a more specific pattern.

## Authentic negative anchors and ordinary alternatives

Use the source passages above as anchors; no new first-person vignette is offered as participant data.

| Published reference/context | Positive feature | Feature-negative boundary / viable alternative |
|---|---|---|
| S1 typical report | Content and attribute-specific presentation/access separability | Its shape answer does not establish a positive shape experience. The reported lack of shape experience is explicit, not an omission inferred from a model. Guessing and scale instructions can explain the answer/status combination. |
| S1 Table 2 interview generalization | Graded clarity/presentation vocabulary | A category describing stimulus occurrence without identifiable content fails `C`; it does not imply absence of all experience. Categories alone cannot instantiate a large prose corpus. |
| S2 S4 training quotation | Content emergence and attention relation | It supplies no attribute-certainty estimate, contrast comparison or recovery probability. Attention capture and retrospective task narration fit the report. |
| S2 aggregate readiness descriptions | Preparatory state and metacognition | Preparedness alone supplies no identified perceptual content or content-specific manner. Do not copy an entire cluster label into every constituent trial. |
| S3 published response words with confidence | Identified content and response confidence | Content-confidence pairs by themselves fail `M`/`A`; they can still be authentic human experience reports. No universal consciousness criterion follows. |
| S4/S5 documented constrained judgments | Attribute comparison and response alternatives | A cue-dependent answer shift can arise through decision criteria. A changed answer cannot automatically become a changed appearance claim. |

Information-matched technical descriptions, ordinary diagnostic narrators, generic uncertainty/memory accounts, and source-grounded model descriptions are essential comparators for **specificity**. They may match `P`; parity limits discrimination from those alternatives without cancelling an independently measured structural correspondence. A changed display or sensor quality can explain appearance vocabulary as well. An internally located referent is not automatically the proposed experiencing locus, and a decision-relevant external device is still external.

## Concrete execution and remaining shortfall

1. Freeze this source/version ledger, definitions, quote-first annotation record, episode eligibility, ambiguity policy and scope axes before generating reports. Archive file hashes at freeze. Reference eligibility comes from documented task/report opportunities, not the observed target text or whether the model succeeded.
2. Qualify annotation on these published anchors and explicitly labelled synthetic boundary cases kept out of Reporter prompts/training. Independent AI contexts can annotate; human raters are optional. Synthetic agreement checks application of a rule, not external human-report validity. Retain disagreements and a fixed adjudication procedure.
3. For each eligible fresh report, annotate `C`, `M`, `S`, `A` as explicit/ambiguous/absent with exact evidence and referents. Score explicit `P` conjunctions and the appearance-scope subset separately. Report partial patterns, omissions and ambiguity. Give lower/upper matching rates with ambiguous cases treated as nonmatches/possible matches; keep all eligible episodes in denominators.
4. Run a separate source-aware fidelity audit covering required and unprompted claims. Report faithful structural matches, unsupported functional claims and experiential assertions whose truth is not auditable. A recovery probability cannot verify felt clarity. Do not silently exclude unsupported reports or call such experiential wording verified.
5. Compare information-matched narrators under fixed prompt/development budgets and report episode/model-instance dependence. Freeze numerical acceptance/reliability rules, sample size, meaningful comparator effects and stopping before evaluating fresh reports. This review supplies no certified cutoffs or power.

The available reference anchors make this a concretely scoreable **publication-grounded qualitative correspondence** study without stimulus/native-trial transfer. What is still missing for stronger validation is an independently held-out, systematically sampled narrative reference corpus with authenticated units, a qualified annotation process and a frozen fresh-report experiment. The two main excerpts alone cannot estimate human pattern prevalence, sensitivity/specificity, or a human/model distributional match. Additional source acquisition must use a fixed search/inclusion rule rather than select passages that fit new machine prose.

For the user's theory, a faithful `P`/`A` match can support the restricted premise that the attention-control model makes accessible contents/state describable in forms documented in human consciousness reports. It does not locate qualia by itself. A theory-facing source comparison additionally needs declared predictions and selective interventions/restoration on the proposed model, alongside upstream representation, generic access/uncertainty, Controller and Reporter alternatives. Report dependence on a state can establish report provenance; it does not establish that state's experience. No necessary own-versus-external superiority prediction follows from these publications.

Maintain Reporter as instrumentation, Controller as action selector/executor, and the attention-control model as the proposed source throughout. Missing participant trial bytes remain a gate for trial-level psychophysical comparisons, not a blanket gate on this narrower qualitative endpoint.
